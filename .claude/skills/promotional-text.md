# Promotional Text Expert

Use this skill when users need help creating compelling promotional content, marketing copy, landing pages, README files, product descriptions, or any text designed to attract, engage, and convert readers into users or customers.

## When to Use

- Writing landing page copy for new products or features
- Creating or improving README files to attract users/contributors
- Crafting product descriptions for marketing materials
- Developing value propositions and taglines
- Writing email marketing copy and newsletters
- Creating social proof sections with testimonials
- Drafting press releases and announcements
- Writing about/company pages
- Creating sales page copy
- Developing investor pitch materials
- Writing app store descriptions
- Creating chatbot scripts and in-app messaging
- Writing webinar and event landing pages
- Crafting video scripts and podcast show notes

## What This Skill Does NOT Cover

- Technical documentation (use `api-documentation` skill)
- Legal/compliance text (use `dutch-corporate-law-expert` skill)
- SEO strategy (use `seo-specialist` skill for in-depth SEO, though this skill covers SEO basics for copy)
- UI/UX design (use `ui-ux-expert` skill)
- Actual website development (use `webdesign` skill)

## How to Use This Skill

When invoking this skill, specify:
1. **Content Type** - What you're writing (landing page, README, product description, etc.)
2. **Tone** - Choose from the 25 available tones below
3. **Language** - Choose from the 11 supported languages below (default: English)
4. **Target Audience** - Who will read this content
5. **Key Messages** - Main points to communicate
6. **Call to Action** - What you want readers to do

**Example prompt:** "Write a landing page hero section with an ENTHUSIASTIC tone in Dutch for small business owners, highlighting our automated bookkeeping features, with a CTA to start a free trial."

### Language-Specific Guidelines

**Dutch (Nederlands)**
- **Formal (zakelijk):** Use "u" pronouns, full company names, professional titles
- **Informal (informeel):** Use "je/jij" pronouns, more casual expressions
- **Power words (Dutch):** Gratis, Nieuw, Nu, Direct, Bewezen, Eenvoudig, Exclusief, Bespaar, Ontdek
- **Avoid:** Excessive anglicisms (use "gebruiker" not "user", "gegevens" not "data")
- **CTA examples:** "Start nu", "Probeer gratis", "Ontdek meer", "Aan de slag"

**German (Deutsch)**
- **Formal:** Always use "Sie" in business contexts
- **Power words:** Kostenlos, Neu, Sofort, Bewährt, Einfach, Exklusiv, Sparen, Entdecken, Garantiert
- **Note:** German readers expect thoroughness and detailed information
- **CTA examples:** "Jetzt starten", "Kostenlos testen", "Mehr erfahren"

**French (Français)**
- **Formal:** Use "vous" in business, maintain elegant tone
- **Power words:** Gratuit, Nouveau, Maintenant, Prouvé, Simple, Exclusif, Économisez, Découvrez
- **CTA examples:** "Commencer maintenant", "Essai gratuit", "En savoir plus"

**Spanish (Español)**
- **Regional variations:** Significant differences between Spain, Mexico, Argentina
- **Power words:** Gratis, Nuevo, Ahora, Probado, Fácil, Exclusivo, Ahorra, Descubre
- **CTA examples:** "Empieza ahora", "Prueba gratis", "Descubre más"

**Japanese (日本語)**
- **Keigo levels:** Sonkeigo (respectful), kenjōgo (humble), teineigo (polite)
- **Cultural notes:** Emphasize harmony, reliability, quality over aggressive sales
- **CTA examples:** "今すぐ始める", "無料で試す", "詳しく見る"

- [ ] Localized idioms (not literal translations)

## Copywriting Frameworks

### PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)
```
PROBLEM: Identify the pain point your audience faces
↓
AGITATE: Amplify the consequences of not solving it
↓
SOLUTION: Present your product as the answer
```

**Example:**
> **Problem:** "Spending hours on manual bookkeeping?"
> **Agitate:** "Every hour you spend on data entry is an hour away from growing your business."
> **Solution:** "Boekhouder automates your entire bookkeeping workflow, giving you back 10+ hours every week."

### BAB (Before-After-Bridge)
```
BEFORE: Current painful state
↓
AFTER: Desired outcome/transformation
↓
BRIDGE: How your product gets them there
```

**Example:**
> **Before:** "Drowning in receipts and spreadsheets"
> **After:** "Crystal-clear financial insights at your fingertips"
> **Bridge:** "Boekhouder automatically categorizes, reconciles, and reports—so you always know where you stand."

### AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
```
ATTENTION: Grab with headline/hook
↓
INTEREST: Build with relevant information
↓
DESIRE: Create want with benefits
↓
ACTION: Clear call to action
```

### 4Ps (Promise-Picture-Proof-Push)
```
PROMISE: Make a bold claim
↓
PICTURE: Paint the outcome
↓
PROOF: Provide evidence
↓
PUSH: Urge action
```

### FAB (Features-Advantages-Benefits)
```
FEATURE: What it does
↓
ADVANTAGE: Why that matters
↓
BENEFIT: How it helps the user
```

## Best Practices

### Headlines That Convert

**Proven Headline Formulas:**
1. **How to [Achieve Desired Outcome]** - "How to Cut Bookkeeping Time by 80%"
2. **[Number] Ways to [Solve Problem]** - "5 Ways to Never Miss a Tax Deadline Again"
3. **The Secret to [Desired Outcome]** - "The Secret to Stress-Free Financial Management"
4. **Why [Target Audience] Are [Doing Thing]** - "Why 10,000+ Businesses Are Switching to Automated Bookkeeping"
5. **[Achieve Outcome] Without [Pain Point]** - "Accurate Books Without Manual Data Entry"
6. **Stop [Pain Point]. Start [Benefit]** - "Stop Chasing Receipts. Start Growing Your Business."
7. **[Bold Claim] in [Timeframe]** - "Set Up Your Entire Bookkeeping System in 15 Minutes"
8. **What [Authority/Proof] Taught Me About [Topic]** - "What Managing 1M+ Transactions Taught Us About Automation"

**Headline Testing Checklist:**
- [ ] Is it specific? (avoid vague claims)
- [ ] Does it promise a clear benefit?
- [ ] Is it believable?
- [ ] Does it create curiosity or urgency?
- [ ] Is it under 10 words? (ideal: 6-8)

### CTAs That Drive Action

**High-Converting CTA Patterns:**
| Pattern | Example | Best For |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Action + Benefit | "Start Saving Time" | General conversions |
| Get + Outcome | "Get Your Free Report" | Lead generation |
| Verb + Now | "Try It Now" | Urgency |
| Action + Free | "Start Free Trial" | Low-friction signup |
| Exclusive Access | "Join the Waitlist" | Pre-launch |
| Personalized | "Show Me My Savings" | Calculators/tools |

**CTA Optimization Tips:**
- Use first person ("Start My Free Trial" vs "Start Your Free Trial")
- Add urgency when appropriate ("Start Now - Limited Spots")
- Reduce friction words ("Get Instant Access" vs "Submit")
- Be specific about what happens next

### Value Proposition Structure

**Template:**
```
[Product Name] helps [Target Audience] [Achieve Outcome] by [Key Differentiator],
so they can [Ultimate Benefit].
```

**Example:**
> "Boekhouder helps small business owners maintain accurate books by automating
> categorization and reconciliation, so they can focus on growing their business
> instead of fighting spreadsheets."

### Social Proof Best Practices

**Types to Include:**
1. **Customer Testimonials** - Real quotes with names and photos
2. **Customer Logos** - Recognizable brands (with permission)
3. **Statistics** - "10,000+ businesses trust us"
4. **Ratings/Reviews** - "4.9/5 on G2"
5. **Case Studies** - Quantified success stories
6. **Certifications** - Trust badges, security certificates
7. **Media Mentions** - "As seen in..."
8. **Awards** - Industry recognition

**Testimonial Formula:**
```
[Specific Result] + [Time Frame] + [Emotional Impact]
"We reduced our bookkeeping time from 10 hours to 2 hours per week.
I finally have weekends back with my family." — Name, Title, Company
```

### Benefit-Focused Writing

**Transform Features into Benefits:**

| Feature | → | Benefit |
|---------|---|---------|
| "Automatic bank sync" | → | "Never manually enter a transaction again" |
| "Real-time reporting" | → | "Know exactly where you stand, anytime" |
| "Multi-user access" | → | "Your whole team on the same page" |
| "Cloud-based" | → | "Access your books from anywhere" |
| "Bank-level encryption" | → | "Your data is safer than a bank vault" |

**Benefit Writing Formula:**
```
Because of [Feature], you can [Action], which means [Emotional Benefit].
```

## Persuasive Writing Techniques

### 1. Use Power Words
**Emotional triggers:** Free, New, You, Instantly, Proven, Easy, Guaranteed, Secret, Exclusive, Limited, Save, Discover, Transform, Unlock, Revolutionary

### 2. Create Urgency
- "Limited time offer"
- "Only [X] spots remaining"
- "Offer ends [date]"
- "Join [X] others who already started"

### 3. Reduce Risk
- "30-day money-back guarantee"
- "No credit card required"
- "Cancel anytime"
- "Free trial, no strings attached"

### 4. Use Specific Numbers
- "Reduce time by 73%" (not "reduce time significantly")
- "Join 12,847 businesses" (not "thousands of businesses")
- "Set up in 4 minutes" (not "quick setup")

### 5. Address Objections Proactively
- "Too expensive?" → Show ROI/value
- "Too complicated?" → Emphasize ease
- "Will it work for me?" → Show similar customers
- "Is it secure?" → Highlight certifications

### 6. Use Social Proof Strategically
- Place near CTAs
- Use specific results over generic praise
- Include recognizable names/companies
- Keep testimonials concise

### 7. Write in Second Person
- "You'll save hours" (not "Users save hours")
- "Your business deserves" (not "Businesses deserve")
- Makes it personal and direct

### 8. Front-Load Benefits
- Lead with the benefit, not the feature
- First sentence should hook the reader
- Most important information first

### 9. Use the "Imagine" Technique
- "Imagine never worrying about tax deadlines again..."
- Creates mental ownership
- Engages emotions before logic

### 10. Repeat Key Messages
- Restate main value proposition in different ways
- Use consistent terminology
- Reinforce through headlines, body, and CTAs

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing any promotional text:

### Clarity
- [ ] Is the main message clear within 5 seconds?
- [ ] Is jargon eliminated or explained?
- [ ] Are sentences short and scannable?
- [ ] Is the reading level appropriate (aim for 8th grade)?

### Persuasion
- [ ] Are benefits emphasized over features?
- [ ] Is there a clear value proposition?
- [ ] Are objections addressed?
- [ ] Is there compelling social proof?

### Action
- [ ] Is there one clear call to action?
- [ ] Is the CTA visible and compelling?
- [ ] Is friction minimized?
- [ ] Is urgency created (when appropriate)?

### Credibility
- [ ] Are claims specific and believable?
- [ ] Is proof provided for bold claims?
- [ ] Are testimonials authentic and specific?
- [ ] Is the tone appropriate for the audience?

### Technical
- [ ] Is spelling and grammar correct?
- [ ] Are headlines capitalized consistently?
- [ ] Is formatting mobile-friendly?
- [ ] Are meta descriptions optimized (150-160 chars)?

## Key Principles

1. **Clarity over cleverness** — Be understood, not admired
2. **Benefits over features** — Sell the outcome, not the tool
3. **Specific over vague** — Numbers and details build trust
4. **Customer over company** — Focus on "you," not "we"
5. **Scannable over dense** — Use headers, bullets, white space
6. **Proof over claims** — Show, don't just tell
7. **One CTA per context** — Don't confuse with multiple asks
8. **Test and iterate** — Copy is never "done"

- [ ] Headings use consistent hierarchy

### Common Copywriting Errors to Avoid

**Clarity Issues:**
| Error | Example | Correction |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Passive voice overuse | "Time is saved by the tool" | "The tool saves time" |
| Nominalizations | "The implementation of..." | "Implementing..." |
| Wordiness | "In order to" | "To" |
| Vague pronouns | "This helps with that" | "This feature reduces errors" |
| Jargon overload | "Leverage synergies" | "Work together effectively" |

**Persuasion Killers:**
- **Weak verbs:** Use "cut" not "reduce," "boost" not "improve"
- **Hedging language:** Remove "might," "maybe," "somewhat"
- **Feature-first:** Always lead with benefit, not feature
- **"We" focus:** Shift to "you" focus

### Readability Metrics

**Target Scores:**
| Metric | Target | Tool |
|--------|--------|------|
| Flesch Reading Ease | 60-70 (8th-9th grade) | Hemingway, readable.com |

**Readability Formula:**
```
Flesch Reading Ease = 206.835 - 1.015(words/sentences) - 84.6(syllables/words)

60-70 = Standard (ideal for most web copy)
50-60 = Fairly difficult
30-50 = Difficult (academic)
```

## Troubleshooting

### Problem 1: Copy Doesn't Convert

**Symptoms:**
- High traffic but low conversion rate
- Users leave the page quickly
- CTAs get few clicks

**Causes:**
- Headline doesn't match user intent
- Benefits unclear or buried below the fold
- CTA is weak, unclear, or hidden
- Too much text, not scannable

**Solutions:**

1. **Test your headline:**
```markdown
❌ BAD: "Welcome to Our Platform"
✅ GOOD: "Cut Your Bookkeeping Time by 80%"
```

2. **Lead with benefits above the fold:**
```markdown
❌ BAD: "We offer automated bank synchronization..."
✅ GOOD: "Never manually enter a transaction again."
```

3. **Strengthen your CTA:**
```markdown
❌ BAD: "Submit" / "Click Here"
✅ GOOD: "Start My Free Trial" / "Get My Free Report"
```

**Prevention:**
- Always test headlines with A/B testing
- Get feedback from target audience before launch
- Use heatmaps to see where users drop off

---

### Problem 2: Content Sounds Generic/Bland

**Symptoms:**
- Copy could apply to any competitor
- No memorable phrases or hooks
- Readers don't remember your message

**Causes:**
- Using industry clichés ("innovative solution", "best-in-class")
- Not speaking to specific pain points
- Missing brand voice and personality

**Solutions:**

1. **Replace generic phrases:**
```markdown
❌ GENERIC: "We provide innovative solutions for your business needs"
✅ SPECIFIC: "We automatically categorize 10,000+ transactions per month so you never touch a spreadsheet"
```

2. **Add specificity:**
```markdown
❌ VAGUE: "Save time on bookkeeping"
✅ SPECIFIC: "Reduce bookkeeping from 10 hours to 2 hours per week"
```

3. **Inject brand personality:**
```markdown
❌ BLAND: "Our software helps businesses"
✅ PERSONALITY: "Because life's too short for spreadsheets"
```

**Prevention:**
- Create a brand voice guide
- Maintain a list of banned generic phrases
- Always include specific numbers and results

---

### Problem 3: Translation Sounds Unnatural

**Symptoms:**
- Native speakers say it "sounds off"
- Grammar is correct but phrasing is awkward
- Idioms don't work in target language

**Causes:**
- Direct translation instead of localization
- Missing cultural context
- Using the wrong register (formal/informal)

**Solutions:**

1. **Localize, don't translate:**
```markdown
❌ LITERAL (English → Dutch): "It's a piece of cake" → "Het is een stuk taart"
✅ LOCALIZED: "Het is een fluitje van een cent"
```

2. **Match cultural expectations:**
```markdown
❌ US-style for Germany: "Awesome! Amazing! Incredible!"
✅ German-appropriate: "Zuverlässig. Präzise. Bewährt."
```

3. **Use the right formality:**
```markdown
❌ Too casual for Dutch B2B: "Hey, wil je meer weten?"
✅ Appropriate: "Wilt u meer informatie ontvangen?"
```

**Prevention:**
- Have native speakers review all translations
- Create language-specific style guides
- Research cultural communication preferences

---

### Problem 4: README Doesn't Attract Contributors

**Symptoms:**
- Few stars or forks on GitHub
- No community contributions
- Users don't understand what the project does

**Causes:**
- Missing or unclear project description
- No quick start guide
- Complex setup instructions
- No visual demo

**Solutions:**

1. **Lead with a clear one-liner:**
```markdown
❌ BAD: "# MyProject"
✅ GOOD: "# MyProject\n> Automated bookkeeping for small businesses in 5 minutes"
```

2. **Add a demo immediately:**
```markdown
![Demo](demo.gif)
```

3. **Provide instant gratification:**
```markdown
## Quick Start
\`\`\`bash
npm install myproject
npx myproject init
# You're live in 30 seconds!
\`\`\`
```

**Prevention:**
- Include badges (build status, version, coverage)
- Add "Why use this?" section early
- Show before/after or competitive advantage

---

### Problem 5: Social Proof Seems Fake

**Symptoms:**
- Testimonials feel manufactured
- Users distrust the reviews
- Conversion decreases when adding testimonials

**Causes:**
- Generic praise without specifics
- Missing attribution (name, photo, company)
- Too-perfect reviews (no balance)
- Outdated testimonials

**Solutions:**

1. **Add specifics:**
```markdown
❌ FAKE-SOUNDING: "Great product! 5 stars!"
✅ AUTHENTIC: "We reduced invoice processing from 3 days to 4 hours. ROI in the first month."
```

2. **Full attribution:**
```markdown
❌ ANONYMOUS: "— A Happy Customer"
✅ VERIFIED: "— Maria de Vries, CFO, TechStart BV | Amsterdam"
```

3. **Include balanced feedback:**
```markdown
✅ HONEST: "Setup took longer than expected, but once running, it's been flawless for 6 months."
```

**Prevention:**
- Ask customers for specific results and permission
- Include photos and LinkedIn profiles
- Use third-party review platforms (G2, Trustpilot)

- [ ] 5-second test — is the main message clear immediately?
- [ ] Spell check in target language completed
- [ ] Brand terminology consistent throughout
- [ ] All claims have supporting evidence
- [ ] CTAs are clear and action-oriented
- [ ] Mobile preview reviewed (text not truncated)

### A/B Testing Protocol

**What to Test:**
| Element | Variations | Minimum Traffic |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Headlines | 2-3 variations | 1,000 visitors each |
| CTAs | Button text, color, placement | 500 conversions each |
| Social proof | Testimonial vs stats | 1,000 visitors each |
| Page length | Long vs short form | 2,000 visitors each |

**Testing Process:**
1. Define success metric (conversions, clicks, time on page)
2. Create variations with single element change
3. Run test for minimum 2 weeks
4. Require 95% statistical confidence
5. Document results and learnings

### Review Workflow

**Solo Review:**
1. Write draft
2. Wait 15+ minutes
3. Read aloud
4. Check against tone/brand guidelines
5. Verify facts and claims

**Peer Review:**
1. Share draft with context (audience, goal, tone)
2. Request specific feedback (clarity, persuasiveness, tone)
3. Incorporate feedback
4. Final self-review

**Stakeholder Approval:**
- Marketing: Brand consistency, campaign alignment
- Legal: Claims verification, compliance
- Product: Feature accuracy, roadmap alignment

## Security Considerations

### Data Privacy in Copy

**Do NOT include:**
- Real customer names without explicit written permission
- Specific revenue figures of customers without permission
- Internal metrics or statistics that are confidential
- Pricing of competitors (legal risk)

**Do include (safely):**
- Anonymized results: "A Dutch retail company saved..."
- Ranges: "Customers report 50-80% time savings"
- Aggregated stats: "Average across 500+ users"

### GDPR Compliance for Marketing Copy

**Required elements:**
```markdown
### Sign-up Forms
- Clear description of what they're signing up for
- Link to privacy policy
- Checkbox for marketing communications (not pre-checked)
- Easy unsubscribe mechanism mentioned

### Testimonials
- Written consent from customer
- Right to withdraw consent
- Data retention policy compliance
```

### Preventing Misleading Claims

**Risk areas:**
- "Best" or "only" claims (need substantiation)
- Performance guarantees without conditions
- Price comparisons without methodology
- Health/safety claims without certification

**Safe alternatives:**
```markdown
❌ RISKY: "The best bookkeeping software"
✅ SAFE: "Rated #1 on G2 for ease of use (2025)"

❌ RISKY: "Guaranteed to save you money"
✅ SAFE: "Customers report average savings of €200/month"

❌ RISKY: "50% cheaper than competitors"
✅ SAFE: "Starting at €29/month — see pricing comparison"
```

## Decision Trees

### Which Tone Should I Use?

```
Is it B2B or B2C?
├── B2B
│   ├── Enterprise/Corporate → FORMAL or AUTHORITATIVE
│   ├── Startup/Tech → PROFESSIONAL or CONFIDENT
│   └── Agency/Creative → CONVERSATIONAL or BOLD
└── B2C
    ├── Premium/Luxury → LUXURIOUS or ASPIRATIONAL
    ├── Everyday/Mass market → CONVERSATIONAL or WARM
    ├── Young audience → CASUAL or HUMOROUS
    └── Problem-solving → EMPATHETIC or EDUCATIONAL
```

### Should I Use Long or Short Copy?

```
What is the user's awareness level?
├── Problem-Unaware → LONG (educate about problem first)
├── Problem-Aware → MEDIUM (focus on solution benefits)
├── Solution-Aware → SHORT (differentiate from alternatives)
└── Product-Aware → VERY SHORT (just the CTA and offer)

What is the price point?
├── Free/Low cost → SHORT (low friction needed)
├── Mid-range → MEDIUM (justify the investment)
└── High-ticket → LONG (build trust, address objections)
```

### Which Language Register?

```
What is the context?
├── Dutch (NL)
│   ├── B2B / Professional → Use "u"
│   ├── B2C / Casual brand → Use "je"
│   └── Unsure → Default to "u" (can't go wrong being polite)
├── German (DE)
│   └── Almost always → Use "Sie" (very rarely du in B2B)
└── French (FR)
    ├── B2B → Always "vous"
    └── B2C → Usually "vous", "tu" only for very young/casual brands
```
